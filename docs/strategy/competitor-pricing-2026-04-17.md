# Competitor Pricing Research — PrayerJar For Churches & Ministries

**Date:** 2026-04-17
**Requested by:** PM (for Sprint 17 pricing page rework)
**Researcher:** Research Agent

---

## Executive Summary

PrayerJar's current ladder — Free (25 members / 1 group) → Starter $19 → Pro $49 → Enterprise (custom) — is **price-competitive and below market for a church-facing SaaS product**. Direct prayer-focused competitors cluster in the $10-$40/mo range (Prayer Platform, PrayerMate Platform, Echo+), while broader church-software suites that bundle prayer as a sub-feature start at $67-$199/mo and scale into $500-$1,200/mo (Breeze, Tithe.ly, Subsplash, Pushpay). The pricing risk is not that PrayerJar is too expensive — it is that (a) the $19 Starter sits in an uncomfortable middle: too cheap to signal church-grade and too expensive compared to Prayer Platform's $10 entry and PrayerMate's $12-15 entry, and (b) the **25-member free tier is noticeably tight** versus PrayerMate (free, 25 subscribers but 3 groups) and Planning Center Groups (free, 15 members, full ChMS adjacent). There is also a transparency advantage: PrayerJar publishes its prices, while Subsplash, Pushpay, and Tithe.ly Enterprise all require a demo call, and Pushpay hides all numbers.

## 2. Detailed Competitor Breakdowns

### 2.1 Prayer Platform (prayerplatform.org)

Prayer Platform is a digital prayer wall product from Impact Factors, and it is the closest direct competitor to PrayerJar in positioning. Public search data indicates pricing **starts at $10/month** for an interactive prayer wall for churches and ministries. The pricing page itself is gated enough that I could not extract a full tier ladder, but third-party references indicate a single low-price entry with an Enterprise/custom option behind a contact form. There is no advertised free tier; the product looks like it converts visitors to paid quickly.

Feature highlights versus PrayerJar: Prayer Platform leads on "prayer wall" branding but appears thinner on community/group features, analytics, and pastoral dashboards. There is no advertised Planning Center / ChMS integration. Their main weakness is marketing opacity — a user cannot easily compare what $10 gets them versus $40. For PrayerJar, Prayer Platform's $10 entry is the most aggressive direct anchor in the market and is a real threat to the Starter $19 price point unless Starter clearly does more.

### 2.2 PrayerMate Platform (prayermate.net/platform)

PrayerMate is the strongest mature competitor. The platform ladder is:
- **Small (Free, perpetual):** 1 feed, 25 subscribers, 3 groups, basic stats
- **Medium ($12-15/mo USD):** 2 feeds, 200 subscribers, 15 groups, advanced stats, secure feeds, social integration, 3-mo free trial
- **Large ($30-40/mo USD):** 10 feeds, unlimited subscribers, 25 groups, same advanced features

PrayerMate is a UK charity-run product from Discipleship Tech, which frames its pricing as sustainability-driven rather than profit-driven — this gives them credibility with ministry buyers who distrust VC-backed church software. Feature strengths: secure/encrypted feeds, rich media in prayer requests, and a patron model for funding. Weaknesses: no ChMS integration, no live-event prayer wall, no AI care flagging, no pastoral dashboard. **PrayerMate's free tier (25 subs + 3 groups) is noticeably more generous than PrayerJar's (25 members + 1 group).** Their $12-15 Medium tier also undercuts PrayerJar's $19 Starter while offering more subscribers relative to price.

### 2.3 Echo Prayer (echoprayer.com)

Echo is a consumer prayer app with a light church/group overlay. Free tier covers personal use. **Echo+ is $14.99/year** (not per month) — essentially $1.25/mo — and unlocks creating groups, feeds, and multi-photo uploads. Echo is not a direct competitor to PrayerJar For Churches because it is priced and positioned per-user rather than per-church. But it is a reference point for how cheaply individual prayer features can be delivered, and pastors who try Echo at $15/yr may balk at PrayerJar's $19/mo Starter if the value delta isn't obvious.

### 2.4 Uplift Prayer (upliftprayer.com)

Fully free for individuals and churches. "Open Groups" are provisioned manually by Uplift for ministries. This is a real competitive risk at the low end: a small church (under 25 members) looking at PrayerJar Free might choose Uplift instead for more features, no member cap pressure, and the same $0 cost. Uplift's weakness is that it appears single-founder / limited-support, and there is no paid tier at all, which raises long-term viability concerns for churches that want a product they can invest in.

### 2.5 Subsplash (subsplash.com)

Subsplash is a full church-engagement suite (apps, websites, media, giving, messaging, events). Pricing is **custom and demo-gated**. Public third-party data indicates base platform ~$149/mo, most churches end up at $300-600/mo, and multi-campus/Enterprise runs $700-1,200+/mo. Giving is a separate $0 product with 2.3% + $0.30 processing. They advertise Planning Center-like features in-suite and Rock RMS integration at Enterprise tier. Prayer is a minor feature of their app builder, not a standalone product — Subsplash is a **category-above competitor**: if a church buys Subsplash, they don't need PrayerJar, but Subsplash is 10x the price.

### 2.6 Pushpay (pushpay.com)

Pushpay is primarily a giving + ChMS platform, **not a prayer product**. Their app builder includes a "Prayer Request Form" as a minor feature. Pricing is entirely opaque — demo required for every tier. Third-party data suggests Basic starts ~$199/mo, and Complete (multi-site, per-campus branding) runs $500-999+/mo. They advertise 80+ integrations at no additional fee. Pushpay is **not a real head-to-head competitor** for PrayerJar — their prayer functionality is an afterthought — but they are the incumbent a large church may already have, and pastoral staff there will ask "why add PrayerJar when Pushpay has a form?" The PrayerJar answer needs to be: live-event prayer wall, AI care flagging, pastoral dashboard, community/groups, none of which Pushpay provides.

### 2.7 Tithe.ly (tithe.ly)

Tithe.ly's published ladder:
- **Free Giving:** $0/mo + 2.9% + $0.30 card fees
- **Church Management:** $72/mo (ChMS + giving)
- **All Access:** $119/mo (ChMS + giving + app + website + worship tools; regularly $228)
- **Enterprise:** custom, for 2,000+ attendee churches
- Premium onboarding $599 one-time

Tithe.ly is transparent about pricing, which PrayerJar should emulate. Prayer is not a named feature — it would live inside the Church App or ChMS as part of communication/groups. Like Pushpay, Tithe.ly is an incumbent competitor by proximity, not by feature parity.

### 2.8 Planning Center (planningcenter.com)

The most important indirect competitor. Planning Center Groups and Planning Center People both have **free tiers** (People: unlimited database free; Groups: 15 members free). Groups scales up to $179/mo in 6 tiers based on active members. Prayer requests are stored as a field in People/Groups, not as a dedicated feature. Because Planning Center is the de facto ChMS for mid-size US Protestant churches, **PrayerJar's integration roadmap must include Planning Center** or pastors will say "we already have this." None of the direct prayer competitors integrate with PCO either — this is a differentiation opening.

## 3. PrayerJar Positioning Assessment (~300 words)

**Where PrayerJar fits:** PrayerJar is positioned in the "purpose-built prayer product" segment alongside Prayer Platform ($10), PrayerMate Platform ($12-40), Echo ($15/yr), and Uplift (free). This segment is the correct segment — churches looking for prayer-specific capability, not a full ChMS. PrayerJar is NOT competing with Subsplash/Pushpay/Tithe.ly head-on; those are 3-10x the price and churches buy them for giving + apps + websites, with prayer as a bundled afterthought.

**Is the $19/$49 ladder in line?** Directionally yes, but the **Starter $19 is mispriced**. Prayer Platform anchors the entry at $10, PrayerMate Medium at $12-15. At $19, PrayerJar Starter needs to visibly do more than either — which today it does not, on paper (150 members vs PrayerMate's 200 at a lower price). The Pro $49 tier is well-positioned: it sits cleanly between PrayerMate Large ($30-40) and the lowest all-in-one ChMS tier (Breeze $67, Tithe.ly ChMS $72). Pro's differentiators — live event prayer wall, AI care flagging, pastoral dashboard — are genuinely novel versus every competitor surveyed, and the $49 price is defensible for those features.

**Pricing advantages:** Published prices (vs Subsplash/Pushpay opacity); unlimited in Pro tier (vs PrayerMate's 10 feeds cap); AI care flagging and live prayer walls are category-unique; Enterprise custom is standard and not a weakness.

**Pricing disadvantages:** (1) Free tier (25 members / 1 group) is tighter than PrayerMate's free (25 / 3 groups) — losing the "just try it with one small group" motion. (2) Starter at $19 is caught between Prayer Platform's $10 and PrayerMate Medium's $12-15 with no obvious feature justification yet. (3) No integrations advertised — every competitor at $49+ advertises at least one (PCO, Rock RMS, QuickBooks). (4) Annual pricing is not advertised; every serious competitor offers 15-20% annual discount.

**Is the free tier competitive?** Weakest as currently sized. PrayerMate gives 25 subs + 3 groups free forever. Uplift gives everything free. Planning Center Groups gives 15 members free with a real ChMS behind it. 25 members / 1 group feels stingy and may cost trial-to-paid conversions.

## 4. Top 3 Recommendations for Strategist + PM

### Recommendation 1 — Loosen the free tier to 50 members / 3 groups
Current free tier (25 / 1) is the least generous in the direct-competitor set. Raising to **50 members / 3 groups** matches or beats PrayerMate, lets a small group or a new church plant actually use the product through the first 6 months of growth, and removes the "25 is too few to even pilot" objection. This won't cannibalize Starter because any church past ~50 members will already feel group-limit pressure and want Pro-tier features (dashboard, live wall). The cost to PrayerJar is negligible — free tier users are already not paying, and the real conversion trigger is the group limit and Pro features, not the seat count.

### Recommendation 2 — Add annual pricing at ~17% discount and make Starter $15/mo on annual
Publish annual pricing for Starter and Pro (`$15/mo billed annually` / `$41/mo billed annually`). This does three things: (a) it gets Starter under Prayer Platform's $10 anchor in commitment terms and under PrayerMate Medium's $12-15, removing the "why not the cheaper option" objection for the buyer who's willing to commit; (b) annual commitments are standard in church SaaS and signal seriousness to finance teams; (c) it improves unit economics because churn on annual is materially lower. Monthly stays at $19/$49 as the casual-trial price.

### Recommendation 3 — Advertise a Planning Center integration on the pricing page, even as "coming soon"
Planning Center is the ChMS most mid-size US churches already have, and **no direct prayer competitor integrates with it**. This is a concrete differentiation wedge at the Pro tier. Even a "Planning Center sync — coming Q3" label on the Pro column will (a) position PrayerJar as church-ops-literate rather than a generic app, (b) reduce the "we already have this in PCO" objection, and (c) give sales/marketing a clean story versus PrayerMate. This costs roadmap commitment, not pricing-page copy — but the pricing page is where it belongs, because integrations are a tier-justifier.

## Risks to Flag

- **Recency uncertainty:** Pricing data above was captured 2026-04-17. Prayer Platform pricing in particular came from third-party summary rather than direct page extraction (their pricing page did not yield clean data via WebFetch) — Architect/PM should verify the $10 anchor by visiting prayerplatform.org/pricing in-browser before using it as a hard comparison in external marketing.
- **"Praytell" product does not appear to exist** under that name — the original request may have been referring to PrayerMate, which is the app that best fits that description. This report treats PrayerMate as the intended competitor.
- **Integration claims cost roadmap:** Advertising a Planning Center integration "coming" creates pressure to ship it. Do not advertise unless Architect has a rough cost estimate and the team accepts the commitment.
- **Free-tier expansion has a conversion-rate risk:** Moving from 25→50 members may let more churches linger on free. Mitigation is to tie group limits and advanced features (AI care, live wall, dashboard) to paid tiers so that a growing church still hits a real upgrade wall.
- **Pushpay/Subsplash/Tithe.ly are not direct competitors but are ubiquitous:** Pricing-page copy should avoid trying to compare to them feature-for-feature; position PrayerJar as complementary to ChMS, not a replacement.
